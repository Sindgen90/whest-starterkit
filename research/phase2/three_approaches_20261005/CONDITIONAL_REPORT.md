# Approach 3: important-direction conditional propagation

**Not submission-ready.** The standalone implementation and random-direction control return finite full-layer predictions and pass official small-network contract validation, but both fail the unchanged full-network residual-overhead gate. They also lose the adjusted objective even if overhead were waived. No archive is created for either.

For a unit input direction u and independent Gaussian z and X_perp,

\[X=uz+X_\perp,\qquad z\sim N(0,1),\quad X_\perp\sim N(0,I-uu^T).\]

The covariance I-uu^T is singular of rank 1023, and retains all perpendicular input variance. No inverse, Cholesky factor, dimension removal or zero-variance substitute is used. At each z node the same full joint closure starts at mean uz, covariance I-uu^T and zero higher input cumulants. We approximate

\[E[f(X)]\simeq{2\over3}\mu_{cond}(0)+{1\over6}\mu_{cond}(\sqrt3)+{1\over6}\mu_{cond}(-\sqrt3).\]

These three standard-Gaussian Gauss-Hermite nodes reproduce the first five scalar polynomial moments. Conditional network means are not polynomials, so this is not an exact conditional integration. We return weighted means for every layer. The first unconditional row is overridden by the exact known Gaussian formula ||W_1[:,j]||/sqrt(2*pi), avoiding unnecessary quadrature error in that row; this override does not alter the states inside the three passes.

[Zahm et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.07922), Proposition 2.3, gives the Gaussian conditional-expectation representation. Proposition 2.5, equations (2.9)–(2.10), bounds ridge approximation error using H=E[J_f^T R_V J_f]; Proposition 2.6, equations (2.12)–(2.13), selects eigen-directions. Our R_V is proportional to the identity for final-layer outputs. We estimate H with eight Gaussian input probes and four independent Gaussian output VJPs per input, never a full Jacobian. If G contains the 32 gradient rows, its leading right singular vector is found from the 32×32 Gram eigensystem and normalized. Preparation and all three closure passes are charged. A zero-gradient case uses a normalized random fallback. ReLU has almost-everywhere derivatives and Gaussian square-integrable gradients; applying the smooth-function result requires its Sobolev extension. More critically, our conditional means are a truncated moment closure and three-node quadrature, not the paper's exact conditional expectation, and the sampled H is not exact. We assert no certified error bound from its discarded eigenvalues.

The random control uses a normalized Gaussian vector selected without reference targets. Both use estimator seeds 20261211/12 on each identical public network, RNG seed plus 1009*mlp.seed. No direction was selected from measured errors. We do not combine these passes with the surrogate or output-sensitive retention.

For a deterministic conditional preactivation, the implementation uses exact ReLU raw moments and zero central cumulants/covariance rows. The first-layer mask recognizes variance within 1e-7 times weight-column norm squared as roundoff-level zero; later exactly zero incoming variances also take the deterministic branch. Other incoming variances use the baseline floor. Final deterministic neurons return ReLU of their mean directly. These branches avoid interpreting a Dirac marginal as a Gaussian with artificial 1e-12 variance. Numerical tests verify Cu=0, PSD in real arithmetic, restoration of I when adding u*u^T Var(z), and exact deterministic-coordinate propagation through three identity layers. Float32 representation can leave roundoff-level covariance eigenvalues. Covariance after a nonlinear closure still has no general PSD/existence guarantee.

There are two distinct additional approximation errors: numerical integration along u, and inherited propagation error within the perpendicular Gaussian law. Small identity-network testing exposed drift for uncertain coordinates under repeated ReLUs, despite correct deterministic coordinates; this is an inherited closure error, not corrected by the decomposition. The existing cumulant audit's local-versus-accumulated diagnostics remain applicable. We did not generate more large-network conditional reference moments or tune nodes because observed adjusted accuracy and official feasibility are already unfavorable. Batching three passes could lower overhead, but cannot remove their measured FLOP penalty and does not justify another candidate here.
